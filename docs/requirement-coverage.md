# Coverage matrix: user-created alerts for important world events

**Pre-plan evidence for the original alert-setting/world-event requirement only.** This matrix tests what is known versus what still needs to work. It is not `ctx/goals/tasks.md` and it is not a claim that the full product is implemented. After the human approves and freezes the plan, the per-deliverable task matrices must cover every agreed requirement. No event-source registration or API key was used.

The human approved worldwide coverage, major disaster types across all regions, breaking news, market movements, and room for other important events. For this in-house, non-monetized project, “important” is provisionally analogous to prominent items in a CNN feed or Reddit trending list. The human asked to gather and **actually use** sources rather than choose based on speculative licensing; live read-only probes below test access, not production operation. Email, Slack, and admin functionality are outside this *first-requirement* matrix.

## Worldwide coverage and “important” — candidate expansion, not approved scope

Scores are provisional editorial judgments on a 0–1 scale, clamped with `max(0, min(1, estimate))` and shown to two decimals; they are for human review, **not measured facts**. “Probed” means a one-off keyless response, not operational ingestion. “Lead” means unvalidated for this use. No blacklisted provider appears as an active source.

**Candidate relevance signals (not a finalized algorithm):** placement in a trusted outlet's prominent feed; provider severity (GDACS/IFRC); independent outlet convergence; scale of human impact; cross-border consequences; and the user's chosen topic/region. A trending count or a headline alone is not proof of truth or importance. No automatic importance ranking or news clustering has been implemented.

**Newly visible gaps:** AI/security items were sampled, but acquisitions, systemic breaches and global critical-infrastructure impact were not established; Nature returned publications but no proof of lasting invention/breakthrough impact. Diplomacy and climate/energy policy lack dedicated event samples; politics/conflict are sampled but not globally comprehensive; world feeds are English-language and editorially selective. Event-level deduplication, multilingual/geographic bias and the alert-authoring flow remain undefined. These are discovery gaps to evaluate with the human, not silently approved new requirements.

## Blacklist: no retries, use an alternative

| Service | Failed probe | Alternative to investigate/use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GDELT DOC | First article request: HTTP 429. A delegated agent **later queried GDELT again despite an explicit blacklist instruction**; that result is excluded and must not be used. No further GDELT calls. | BBC/NYT world RSS and additional independent keyless news feeds. |
| ReliefWeb v1 | `/disasters` returned HTTP 410. Earlier research also identified access friction in v2; do not pursue this provider. | WHO DON and IFRC GO for complementary humanitarian/health data. |
| Meteoalarm | Probed warning-feed endpoint returned HTTP 404; do not retry that service during this research. | GDACS, EONET, IFRC GO and other verified providers. |
| Stooq | Probed CSV price endpoint returned HTTP 404; do not retry that service. | Look for documented keyless equity/index data; the Yahoo sample is not yet a validated replacement. |

**Interpretation:** an HTTP 200 response proves only that a single keyless request returned data; it does not prove that the product has ingested it continuously, that every major disaster type and region is covered, that news is universally breaking, that markets are globally represented, or that an alert can already be configured. An in-house, noncommercial project still needs to respect source conditions, but source-rights investigation should not replace gathering and using working data. Blacklisted services must not be probed again.

**New catch-up validation need:** the human requests a 24-hour lookback on application startup for important events across **all defined categories** (news, markets, disasters, and others), and normal notification of late-reported important items without an overwhelming burst. One-off responses from editorial feeds and market/disaster endpoints do not establish that all sources expose important items from the prior 24 hours or event-occurrence timestamps; validate historical availability and geographic coverage by category before claiming catch-up completeness. No blacklisted source should be retried.
