# World-event alerts: exploratory concept

**Status: discussion draft, not a frozen design or plan.** The human has approved the event-coverage matrix and the local runtime direction described below, but not final alert behavior, service levels, or source completeness. See `ctx/goals/plan.md` for the original requirement and agreed human direction.

## Given goals

Users set up alerts for important world events, including breaking news, market movements, and natural disasters. Notifications work via email and Slack; the delivery design allows future channels. An admin view is required. This is a greenfield product, with no imposed stack or existing system. All three event families and both initial channels belong in the eventual deliverable; a single-source demo would not meet the stated goals.

**Human clarification:** worldwide coverage of major disaster types across all regions is required; do not equate one example feed's limits with product limits. Breaking news and market movements remain required, and “important” is intentionally open to additional event categories. Investigate aggregators, including GitHub-hosted projects. Do not register for event-data provider services or API keys; this restriction does **not** extend to email/Slack delivery services. A candidate's availability alone does not establish comprehensive coverage or permission to redistribute alerts; those require evidence before the plan is frozen.

**Further clarification:** this is an in-house, non-monetized project. The human approved the stated goal and wants source collection and working use prioritized. For now, think of “important” as relevant/prominent material surfaced by a CNN-style feed or Reddit trending, rather than a universal mathematical score. Evidence for the original requirement is tracked in `docs/requirement-coverage.md`; derive the full plan only after the human reviews that coverage, and make `ctx/goals/tasks.md` cover the frozen plan afterward. The no-registration restriction remains limited to event-data providers.

## Product interpretation to review

**Agreed local runtime direction (no implementation yet):** each person runs their own copy locally, not through GitHub hosting. C# application with a frontend for alert setup and admin, SQLite persisted across restarts, SMTP email and Slack incoming webhooks (delivery secrets must be configured privately), and a containerized startup path invoked by Linux/Windows scripts placed in the repository's top-level directory (not scripts requiring root/administrator privileges). Launch sequence is build → test → run, with no GitHub Actions; rerunning must preserve saved data and avoid duplicate running pollers or repeat notifications for already-delivered items. On startup, look back over the previous 24 hours for important events in **all defined categories** (news, markets, disasters, and others) not yet notified, including items from when the computer was off; treat late-reported items as normal notifications while avoiding a burst. The prior unconditional under-five-minute-from-occurrence/skip-offline direction is superseded for those cases. Docker availability/access, credentials, and a powered-on host are prerequisites. A single ASP.NET Core service serving pages and doing background collection/matching, with a persistent Docker volume, is a lightweight implementation candidate; its concrete structure and security behavior still need review. Local startup does not imply public internet access.

**Agreed alert setup:** subscribe to an event type from the approved coverage matrix and choose notification channels; receive notifications when an important event in that type occurs. Per-instrument, location, threshold, or other custom conditions are **not agreed requirements**. Whether subscriptions can be paused/edited, how delivery history is shown, and the exact admin actions remain to be decided. Source adapters, importance assessment and delivery adapters are candidate internals, but a mechanism to add channels for platforms not pre-coded in the app **without production-code changes** must be designed and reviewed. Deterministic metadata-informed importance scoring is a goal, not an implemented formula.

"Important" cannot be one universal classifier without making unreliable promises. Each domain needs an explicit definition: e.g., a provider's warning severity, a user-specified price/percentage threshold, or a news topic with editorial/relevance criteria. Revisions, duplicate stories, cancellations, time zones, delivery retries, and stale-source warnings affect the value of the alert and should be scoped deliberately rather than hidden.

## Narrow feasibility findings

| Area | Grounded finding | Product consequence / uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earthquakes | [USGS GeoJSON feeds](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/geojson.php) publish updated global event data with IDs, magnitude, coordinates, timestamps and revisions; summary feeds refresh every minute. | A credible earthquake signal exists; revised events need deduplication and an update policy. This does not cover all disasters. |
| Severe weather | [NWS alerts](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-alerts) expose US watches/warnings/advisories, recommend polling no more than every 30 seconds, and permit third-party redistribution. They are not a worldwide all-hazards source. | One supplementary source among many; its regional scope does **not** narrow the worldwide product goal. |
| Breaking news | [BBC World RSS](https://feeds.bbci.co.uk/news/world/rss.xml), [NYT World RSS](https://rss.nytimes.com/services/xml/rss/nyt/World.xml), [Al Jazeera English RSS](https://www.aljazeera.com/xml/rss/all.xml) and [Guardian World RSS](https://www.theguardian.com/world/rss) returned keyless article lists. GDELT was blacklisted after HTTP 429. | Conflict/war, politics/elections, and other consequential topics are world news, not natural hazards; editorial curation offers relevance but not complete coverage. |
| Market movements | Market feeds vary by instrument, venue, delay, and commercial redistribution rights; a free API listing is **not evidence** of production rights to send price alerts to third parties. Candidate documentation to validate next: [Finnhub](https://finnhub.io/docs/api), [Massive](https://massive.com/pricing), [Twelve Data](https://twelvedata.com/docs). | No market-data provider or latency guarantee selected. Confirm exact instrument scope, licensing, data freshness, and alert redistribution rights before freezing the plan. |
| Slack | [Slack incoming webhooks](https://docs.slack.dev/messaging/sending-messages-using-incoming-webhooks/) can post to one selected channel; URLs are secrets and a distributed app requires an installation/OAuth flow. Sent webhook messages cannot be deleted by webhook. | Choose installation and permissions model for real users, secret handling, and error recovery; a manually pasted webhook is not automatically the right product experience. |
| Email | No sender, domain, provider, or deliverability requirement is specified. | Confirm production sender identity, verification, abuse controls and bounce handling before promising reliable delivery. |

These are directional source checks, not a guarantee that any provider will authorize the eventual commercial/use case. No credentials, providers, or external integrations have been configured.

**Delivery credentials are separate from data-source keys.** Per [Slack's incoming-webhook guide](https://docs.slack.dev/messaging/sending-messages-using-incoming-webhooks/), a workspace user can authorize a webhook for one channel. Sending then uses its unique secret URL, not a Slack API key; the URL must still be stored privately and can be revoked. For a distributed, self-service app, Slack documents an OAuth installation flow that can produce webhooks, so “no API key” does not mean “no Slack configuration or authorization.” Email can use an authenticated SMTP relay or an email service; reliable delivery still requires an authorized sender and operational setup. The exact delivery integrations remain undecided.

## Plausible product shapes (unselected)

1. **Source-specific alerts:** precise per-domain rule forms and trusted signals; easier to explain, but heterogeneous user experience and extra source integrations.
2. **One general “anything important” prompt:** simple surface, but high ambiguity, false positives, unclear copyright/coverage, and hard-to-verify promises.
3. **Hybrid:** a shared alert/delivery experience with explicit domain-specific conditions. This is the working hypothesis because it retains precision without duplicating user/channel/admin foundations; it still needs human approval and validated sources.

## Boundaries to settle before the plan is frozen

1. Treat global coverage as a requirement. Define a checkable taxonomy of major disaster types and a disaster-type × geography coverage matrix; identify enough lawful keyless sources to cover it, and disclose verified gaps without downgrading the requirement by default.
2. Define conditions and latency/accuracy promises for "breaking," "market movement," and "important," including duplicates and updates.
3. Validate keyless source contracts, production access, attribution and redistribution rights, costs, operational limits, and fallback sources across **all** required news, market and disaster signals. If the no-registration constraint and worldwide coverage genuinely conflict, bring evidence back for a human decision rather than changing either silently.
4. Decide the real user and admin journeys: identities, tenant/workspace separation, rule ownership, Slack install/connection, email verification, admin authority and auditability.
5. Define acceptance evidence: real end-to-end alerts for each of the three event families, both channels, failure/retry behavior, admin oversight, and an added channel without rewriting matching logic; specify what can be verified safely with synthetic events versus live integrations.
6. Decide deployment, security, data retention, monitoring, budget, and what credentials/accounts the human can supply for real external delivery tests.
