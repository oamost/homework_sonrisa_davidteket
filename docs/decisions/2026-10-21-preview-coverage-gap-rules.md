# Mixed public preview and consequential-news criteria (implementation handoff)

The existing `/api/news/important` route now uses the same eligibility, classification and scoring path as `/api/alerts/selection`; it remains read-only and does not consult subscriptions. Its name remains for route compatibility. Each displayed event carries its actual graded importance decision score (finite 0–1 float), rule ID and factors. The UI hides these until “Show score debug” is enabled and formats the score to two decimals. Scores are **engineering metadata-strength rankings**, not probabilities or calibrated certainty. `IsImportant` remains determined by the previously approved boolean triggers, never a numeric score cutoff. Rejected market observations are not silently scored important: the response counts only eligible observations with a supported 24-hour interval lacking an exact baseline, even when rejected diagnostic entries are capped.

### Graded strength heuristics (not human-approved universal weights)

- News: tier-1 urgency headline 0.70 plus 0.05 with usable provider publication time; two independent tier-1 same-story reports within six hours 0.85; consequential subject/action headline 0.65; no trigger 0. These grades distinguish evidence quality but cannot verify a story.
- Disaster: GDACS Orange 0.80, Red 0.95, tsunami flag 0.90; earthquake magnitude at 6 starts 0.70 and rises linearly by 0.25 over the next three magnitude units (capped); IFRC severity at 2 starts 0.65 and rises linearly by 0.25 over the next three levels (capped). Use the highest available approved matched signal, otherwise 0. These are bounded descriptive strengths, **not hazard likelihoods**.
- Market: qualifying move starts 0.60 at the approved family-specific threshold, adding at most 0.30 from excess relative move `(ratio−1)/(ratio+1)` and at most 0.05 from remaining freshness `1 − age/(2 × interval)`. Below-threshold valid fresh moves receive at most `0.45 × move/threshold` and remain not important; invalid/stale/missing inputs receive 0. Raw approved threshold comparison and exact baseline rules are unchanged. No fabricated baseline or universal cross-market comparability is implied.

## Additional deterministic news rule

Preserve the approved tier-1 headline-urgency rule first, and the independent tier-1 same-story six-hour rule second. A further `news.consequential-topic` rule uses an explicit *headline subject plus consequential action*, with no invented numeric weight: government/president/parliament/election + resigns/ousted/dissolves/election result; treaty/sanctions/ceasefire/diplomatic relations + signed/imposed/agreed/suspended/lifted; ransomware/cyberattack/data breach/hackers + shutdown/disruption/exposure/breach; AI model/system + ban/mandate/nationwide deployment/recall/regulator approval; acquisition/merger + completion/approval/block/agreement/announcement; power grid/water supply/telecom/hospital network + shutdown/outage/disruption/restoration; oil spill/power plant/emissions/energy grid + shutdown/leak/ban/emergency/mandate; clinical trial/fusion reactor/scientific breakthrough + success/approval/demonstration/achievement; troops/armed forces/ceasefire + invasion/attack/collapse. Opinion, analysis, review, explainer, how-to, sponsored and promotional headlines do not satisfy this additional topic rule. Metadata-only signals cannot verify factual importance or truth; a matching headline can still be false, ironic, routine or misleading. No free-standing feed category, outlet name, or topic noun alone promotes a story.

Corroboration associates only exact normalized titles with at least four words and twenty characters, with provider publication times no more than six hours apart. Tier-1 source **organizations** (BBC, NYT, Al Jazeera, Guardian) rather than feed IDs count independently; two BBC feeds do not corroborate each other. This conservative check misses paraphrases and may falsely merge recycled identical headlines. There is no persisted cross-poll story cluster, fuzzy matching or fabricated historical coverage.

Specific news subscription codes come from item categories or subject-bearing headline signals, never from a broad feed family alone. Item-level aliases `cyber`, `ai`, `environment`, `transformative-science`, and the other approved topic aliases resolve to their canonical taxonomy codes. GDACS/IFRC/EONET hazard variants map to their canonical disaster codes; generic EONET "Severe Storms" requires cyclone/hurricane/typhoon evidence in the event title before mapping to cyclone. Tsunami flag is also represented separately. Missing topic evidence leaves only `world-news` (and is not a claim the item is globally important).

The mixed feed only displays events with provider-backed 24-hour eligibility and approved family-specific importance. Actual market baselines must match exact source/instrument/quote/interval and 24-hour timestamp. Provider gaps, geographic incompleteness, omitted rejected diagnostics and missing market baselines remain visible; neither market history nor live entries are synthesized. Per-family diagnostics report all evaluated records, provider-time-eligible records and eligible+important records, independent of the display cap. A browser still calling an older running API contract displays "Not available from this API response" instead of a blank or fabricated score. Serialized `dotnet test src/WorldAlerts.sln --nologo` passed 440/440 after the scorer changes; focused landing tests passed 5/5; frontend build succeeds with the pre-existing bundle-budget warning.
